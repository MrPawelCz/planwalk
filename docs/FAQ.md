# FAQ

**Is this just another floor plan editor?** No. The editor problem is solved by others (for example Pascal Editor). planwalk is the missing step before it: reading an existing drawing into clean data, checking it, and turning it into something you can walk through. The JSON is the product; the viewer is one consumer of it.

**Why JSON and not a model file?** Because an AI can write it, a human can read and fix it, a validator can check it and explain errors, and any tool can consume it. The viewer exports GLB for everything else.

**How accurate is the reading?** It depends on the model and the drawing. There are two measurements, both with their results and every caveat in `docs/BENCHMARK.md`: a smoke test on synthetic plans with known ground truth in four drawing styles (the harness to reproduce it is in `eval/`), and a first look at 20 real plans from public listings. On 12 clean synthetic plans Claude Sonnet 5.5, reading by hand in an agent session, scored a mean 99.6 out of 100; read in one call with the first prompt version, the way a person uses a chat, the same plans scored 92.4 and 95.3 (two runs of Claude Sonnet 5.5), 87.8 (gpt-6-astra) and 34.4 (Claude Haiku 4.5, which scored 50.3 with the current prompt version). Those plans are easy and the reader was not blind to the harness, so it says the pipeline works, not that real plans are read this well. On the 20 real plans (10 Polish, 10 UK) the result was mixed, most readings needed corrections: of 30 readings by Claude Sonnet 5.5 (it read the 10 Polish plans twice, with the Polish and with the English prompt, so these are 30 readings of 20 plans), 3 needed no change, 12 needed at most three small corrections, 12 were wrong and 3 did not load; none of the 20 readings by Claude Haiku 4.5 was usable even after small corrections (9 were wrong and 11 did not load). The Polish plans, mostly developer unit cards, read better than the UK agent drawings (for Claude Sonnet 5.5, usable or fixable with small corrections in 13 of 20 readings of Polish plans (10 plans, each read twice) against 2 of 10 of UK plans). That set is small and picked by hand, the truth is what is printed on the drawings (transcribed by AI readers and not checked by a human) and the judge is an AI, so take the figures as a first look; hand sketches without a scale and scans were not measured, so expect worse there. The validator catches structural mistakes, not misreadings: always compare with your drawing, and when a reading is wrong, the "Report a bad reading" button in the viewer opens a prefilled issue.

**Does my plan leave my machine?** planwalk never uploads anything, and the viewer makes no network requests of its own; the one exception is the optional `?plan=<url>` parameter, which downloads a plan from the address you give it. The image goes only to the AI you choose to give it to (your chat or your agent), so cover names, addresses and phone numbers on the drawing first (cropping is enough). The "Report a bad reading" button opens a new GitHub tab only after you click it, and the plan goes to your clipboard, not into the address (more below). The report is a public GitHub issue, so cover names and addresses before you paste the plan.

**Which AI do I need?** One that can read images and write JSON. What is measured: on synthetic plans the models listed in the Runs by model table of `docs/BENCHMARK.md`, and on real plans only Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Haiku 4.5 (the Real listing plans section), with the caveats of each run; Claude Sonnet 5.5 did clearly better than Claude Haiku 4.5 in this test; other models were not measured on real plans (gpt-6-astra and Gemini included). Other chats that read images may work, but that is an expectation, not a result; results are welcome. The prompt in `docs/PROMPT.md` is meant for a plain chat (`docs/PROMPT.pl.md` for Polish drawings, measured on the 10 Polish real plans: with the Polish prompt 9 of 10 readings by Claude Sonnet 5.5 loaded, 2 were usable without changes and 5 needed small corrections; with the English prompt on the same plans 10 of 10 loaded, 1 was usable without changes and 5 needed small corrections; Claude Haiku 4.5 did much worse, with the Polish prompt 6 of 10 loaded and none was usable or needed only small corrections; with one reading per plan, differences of a few points between rows cannot be told apart from noise). The skill in `skill/planwalk` is a standard skill folder for agents that load skills; it has not been benchmarked.

**The result looks wrong. What can I try?** Crop the image to the plan (keep a room table if the drawing has one), cover names and addresses in the title block, and send one floor per image if the drawing is busy. If the viewer lists problems, press "Copy fix request" and paste it back into the same chat. If walls do not meet or rooms look too big, tell the chat: "trace the centerline of the thick wall strips and make walls meet at shared corner points". Compare the result with your drawing: planwalk shows what the JSON says, not what the drawing meant. If that does not help, press "Report a bad reading" (among the buttons of the page, in the Menu on a phone, and in the Issues panel): it opens a prefilled GitHub issue in a new tab, with only the version, the languages and the validator codes with their counts in the address, and the plan goes to your clipboard (when the clipboard fails, it shows in a box you can copy from), to paste after you cover names and addresses. The report is a public GitHub issue, and planwalk itself sends nothing.

**Why no server or build step for users?** `dist/planwalk.html` is one file: double click it. Standalone export embeds your plan in one HTML file, to open on a computer; to send the flat to someone's phone use Copy link (the plan is stored inside the link, so whoever gets the link, and the app you send it through, can read it; a link copied from a downloaded file points to the live page, so opening it needs a connection).

**Does it run without a GPU?** Yes, in software rendering (we test headless Chrome with SwiftShader), just slowly. Frame rates with a real GPU have not been measured.

**Can I use it for real-estate listings?** Yes, with care: it visualises the drawing you give it and is not a survey. Most readings of real plans needed corrections in our measurement (see "How accurate is the reading?"), so check every room against the drawing before you publish a model, and say in the listing that it is a visualisation. Not for permits or construction.

**What about furniture, textures, exterior, roofs?** Furniture is decorative: simple built-in shapes chosen by type (sofa, bed, table, plant and so on), placed where the plan says, with soft rounded forms in the textured look. Textures (wood, tile, carpet, stone, plaster, fabric, linen, knit, rugs, leaves) are generated in code from each room's floor type, the furniture type and a few fixed rules, not read from your drawing; a plain clay look is one click away. No roofs and no terrain in v0.1. Stairs are straight stairs only.

**Why MIT?** So anyone can use it in products. If you do, a link back is appreciated, not required.
